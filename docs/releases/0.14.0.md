Terrain gets tools for the work map makers do by hand: a ramp or a bridge stretched as it
is placed, a brush that mends the joins that show, pins that keep hand-laid ground, and a
report that holds a symmetric map against its own image. A mod's tileset can be laid as
its sheet, and the isometric brush lays the terrain a mod copied in. Bots can be written
in JavaScript. Saving a map no longer makes it larger, and the `scmjs` command line was
gone over from end to end; **What a script will notice** at the end lists the behaviour a
script may rely on. On scmjs.dev the editor is now one of three doors, beside the game and
replays.

## Terrain

- A ramp or a bridge stretches as it is placed. With the doodad picked, press where it
  should stand and drag along it: a ramp gets wider, a bridge longer or wider, one step
  for every two tiles of the drag. Shift offers all four directions. It goes down as
  terrain, in one undo step.
- A paste stretches the same way. With a clip armed, drag instead of clicking and its
  middle is laid again along the drag. A stretched clip pastes its terrain only.
- **View ▸ Seam Overlay** draws a line along every join between two tiles that shows.
  The new **Heal** tab is a brush for those joins: it lays a tile that meets its
  neighbours better and that walks, builds and blocks sight as the old one did. Its
  button heals the marked area or the whole map.
- The new **Pin** tab keeps hand-laid ground. A pinned tile is skipped by the brushes
  that lay terrain by rule (Isometric, Rect, Heal, the fills, Replace Terrain) and still
  written by the Tile and Blend brushes, a paste and a doodad. **Pin All Hand-Laid
  Tiles** covers a map from another editor in one click, and a doodad placed as terrain
  is pinned as it goes down. Pins are saved with the map; the game does not read them.
- Under a left-right symmetry mode a ramp or a bridge is mirrored as its twin, the doodad
  drawn for the mirrored cliff, so both sides get one. The status bar says when the
  game's two do not play alike.
- **Tools ▸ Symmetry…** shows how much of the ground plays alike under each mode, which
  also tells which symmetry a map was made with. For the chosen mode it lists the places
  that differ in walking, height or building, and checks mineral fields, geysers and
  start locations. A place in the list is a button that goes there, and **View ▸
  Symmetry Differences** paints the differences on the map.
- With a doodad picked, a drag on the map no longer draws a selection box. It does again
  once placing is stopped.

## A mod's terrain

- The Tile tab has a **Sheet** view: the tileset in rows of sixteen, so a picture that
  spans several tiles shows whole. Drag across the sheet to pick a block, click on the
  map to lay it, and drag to repeat it as a pattern. Fill Area and Fill Map tile it too.
  It works for the game's own tilesets as well.
- The **Pieces** view lists the parts of the sheet that maps lay whole, cut from the open
  map and, with **Learn from Maps…**, from a folder of maps. Alt+click on the map picks
  a whole piece. A filter shows only the groups a data set adds, and the same tile is no
  longer listed twice.
- The Isometric tab lists a tileset's **Copied terrain**: a terrain the tileset holds
  again at another height with its edges redrawn, which the brush could not lay before.
- **Find terrain from other tilesets**, on the same tab, finds the terrain a tileset
  holds from the game's other tilesets, such as a mod's Badlands that carries
  Installation's floors, and the isometric brush paints it. With **Drawn borders** on, a
  stroke lays the tileset's own border pieces where that ground meets the map's.
- A mod can ship all of this as a `terrain.json` beside its `mod.json`: named terrains,
  pieces, borders, and the tables for copied and borrowed terrain. Everyone who adds the
  mod gets them with nothing to learn or scan. **Save as a terrain file…** in the
  Pieces view writes it.
- The guide's Terrain page shows each of these tools with a picture, and a Mod terrain
  page follows it.

## Bots

- A bot can be written in JavaScript or TypeScript. `scmjs bot run mybot.js <map>` and
  `scmjs bot ladder` play the file as it is, with nothing to build, in the same games as
  BWAPI bots and the game's computer. A TypeScript bot needs Node 22.18 or newer.
- The new guide at [docs.scmjs.dev/bots](https://docs.scmjs.dev/bots/) covers playing
  against a bot, writing a first one from a small bot that works, and bringing a BWAPI
  bot.
- `@scm-js/bot` on npm holds a bot's types, for completion in your own editor.
- `scmjs mod header --js` writes a mod's units, weapons, upgrades and technologies as a
  JavaScript module, under the names a bot is given in that mod's game.

## Saving and opening

- Saving a map that StarEdit made with sounds in it no longer grows the file. Its sounds
  were written back uncompressed, which made such a map about 2.7 times larger on any
  save, even a rename: a 573 KB map became 1.5 MB. Sounds that did not change are now
  written as they were stored, in the editor's Save and in every `scmjs` command that
  writes a map.
- A file named `.scm`, `.scx` or `.chk` that holds no map is refused. It used to open as
  an empty 64 × 64 map, and a command that wrote to it replaced the file.
- Trigger text with a mistake in it, and a file picked in **Import Triggers** and then
  not imported, no longer leave their strings behind in the map.
- Choosing a 1.16.1 folder under **Help ▸ Game Data…** keeps its `Local.dll`, which
  holds the game's keys for control groups and the F2 to F4 screen positions, so they
  work on the play page. A copy made before this says what it lacks: remove it and choose
  the folder again.

## On scmjs.dev

- The site is arranged around three doors: the editor, the game, and replays. On the site
  the editor's menu bar has the other two at its right end, with the game data this
  browser holds and your library, and the name at its left leads to the site's home. The
  desktop app, and an editor run anywhere else, look as they did.
- A map or a replay dropped on the site's home page opens in the editor in the same tab.
  A replay opens in the replay analysis, which is now where the site watches every
  replay; the play page hands its own over.
- With no map open, after the last one is closed, the map's place shows where to drop a
  map, **New map…**, **Open a file…** and the recent files. The editor still starts on a
  blank map.
- With no map open the minimap is blank and the bar no longer shows the last map's name.

## Mods and replays

- Every tool now reads a mod the same way. The editor, the command line, Aftermath, the
  Mod Editor and the play page find the same files in it and give it the same name and
  hash. A mod's hash changed with this: `scmjs mod info` shows the earlier one beside
  the new, and a replay saved under the earlier one still finds its mod.
- A mod that ships as a FireGraft patcher `.exe` is read whole: `scmjs mod info` and
  **Add a data set…** list its plugins in the order the patcher loads them. An exe that
  cannot be read says why; it used to show a mod of no files.
- For a replay of a mod whose code ran, Aftermath's Game view shows the game's own
  screen: its console, minimap, tooltips and the mod's own interface, drawn by the game.
  **Game's own screen** on the timeline switches back to the play page's picture and the
  editor's overlays.
- The Mod Editor (0.6.0, under **Plugins ▸ Browse Plugins…**) hands the play page the
  mod's code with **Play** and **Start Test Game**, and saves a mod that came as one
  program as that program. Before, its plugins were left out.
- TrigScript 4.5.0 has two new libraries: `trigscript/workers`, where a worker rallied to
  minerals or gas starts gathering, and `trigscript/spells`, where a Medic restores an
  afflicted unit nearby. A unit takes `gather`, `cast` and `stop`, and `distanceTo`
  measures between two units.
- At scmjs.dev, the feature counts now say which tool made an edit and which overlay was
  switched, as words from a fixed list. **Preferences ▸ Privacy** turns counting off.

## The command line: work that could be lost

- `scmjs map triggers import` refuses a file with no triggers in it, which used to remove
  every trigger of the map. `--allow-empty` says it is meant.
- `scmjs mod unpack` into a folder that already holds an unpacked mod is refused unless
  `--force`. It used to write over the folder, and what had been changed there was gone.
- `map triggers edit` and `map strings edit` keep your text when the map cannot be
  written, and say the command that brings it in.
- `map unpack` no longer mixes two maps in one folder: a folder of another map, or one
  whose files changed since it was unpacked, is refused unless `--force`.
- The tools `scmjs mcp` gives an AI refuse an argument they do not know. A misspelt
  `dryRun` used to be dropped, and the map was written.

## The command line: what was broken

- Stopping a command stops the game it started. A game kept running at full speed after
  the command was killed, cancelled by CI or cut short by a pipe; temporary folders were
  left behind too.
- `map test record` takes `--json`, `--frames` and `--starcraft`; it ignored all three.
- A misspelt key in a tests file is refused, with the nearest key it knows. Such a test
  used to pass, or check the opposite of what was meant.
- `map rush` on a map with units placed on it reports the walks of the unit asked for;
  it listed hundreds that were not.
- `map diff --rev A..B` is refused, where it reported the whole map as added;
  `--rev main...` compares with where the branch left main. `map log` lists commits in
  git's order.
- A replay saved from a game of a mod by `bot ladder`, `map test --mod` or `mod test`
  says which mod it was.
- `bot run` reads its values and its files before the game starts; `--frames abc` ran no
  game and reported success.
- `map pack --out -` and `mod pack -o -` write to standard output; they wrote a file
  named `-`.
- `scmjs doctor` starts the browser it finds, and says when it does not start.
- A missing file, a folder where a file is wanted and the like are said in one line; about
  ten of them printed an internal error.
- Shell completion offers options after a file name, the values an option takes, and the
  commands it lacked. Write the script again: `scmjs completion bash`.

## The command line: one way of doing things

- `-o` is `--out` on every command that writes, and `-` is standard output wherever one
  file is written.
- `--force` means writing over what is there, and nothing else. Taking replays of another
  map in `map heatmap` and `map coverage` is `--any-map`; packing a mod despite errors in
  `mod build` is `--ignore-errors`.
- `--all` means the whole list. The whole-map camera of `replay export` is `--whole-map`.
- `--size` is a map's size. A clip's size in `replay export` is `--resolution`, and the
  picture's size in `replay watch` is `--cells`.
- `--frames` takes a number of frames or a time such as `1:40`, and a race is its name or
  its first letter, in every command.
- With `--json`, standard output is the JSON and nothing else, and a file is named as it
  was typed.
- What a command says while it works goes to standard error, so `> report.txt` holds the
  report alone.

The old spellings of the renamed options still work.

## New on the command line

- `scmjs map --help` and `scmjs mod --help` list their commands by what they are for.
- `--dry-run` on `map set`, `map resize`, `map pack` and both imports; `--json` on both
  imports and both edits.
- `map dump` lists sprites, doodads and the settings that differ from a new map's, and
  gives each section a hash, so painted terrain shows in `git diff`.
- `mod new --tables units,weapons` starts a mod from the game's own tables as text.
- `mod info` lists a mod's `terrain.json`, `mod check` checks it and `mod unpack` keeps
  it.
- `map resize` lists what it drops, and names a start location among them.
- `scmjs help map diff` works, and a mistake in a command line names the help that lists
  that command's options.
- A release now reaches Homebrew and Scoop when it is published; they used to find it
  hours later.
- The guide at [docs.scmjs.dev/cli](https://docs.scmjs.dev/cli/) opens with a first look
  at five commands and their output, and setting `scmjs mcp` up for an AI tool has a page
  of its own.

## What a script will notice

- A mistake in the command line, a missing file and a file of the wrong kind exit with 2.
  Some of these exited with 1.
- `map dump` has a new form, so every map shows once in a `git diff` set up by
  `scmjs init`. If git keeps showing the old form, clear what it kept:
  `git update-ref -d refs/notes/textconv/scmjs`.
- `map optimize --keep` is given once for each member: `--keep a --keep b`.
- `mod diff a b` exits with 1 when the two mods differ, as `map diff` does.
- `map trace … | head` no longer ends the game early: its progress is on standard error.
- `-v` after a command is an unknown option. Before, it printed the version and the
  command did not run.
- `scmjs mcp` reads and writes only inside the folder it was started in.
  `--allow-outside` lifts that.
- A folder `map unpack` wrote with an earlier version may ask for `--force` once.
- A mod's hash is the new one. Every `--json` field of 0.13.3 is still there.

## For plugin authors

- The terrain tools are on the API. In a transaction: `tx.placeStretchedDoodad`,
  `tx.heal`, `tx.pin`, `tx.placeBlock`, `tx.fillPattern`, `tx.paintBorrowed` and
  `tx.paintCopy`. Reads: `api.terrain.seamStep`, `seams`, `pinned`, `handLaid`,
  `symmetryShares`, `fairness`, `sheet`, `pieces`, `pieceAt`, `borrowed` and `copies`,
  and `api.palette.stretchAxes` and `doodadTwin`.
- Pins hold for plugins too. A transaction's rule-based writes (`paintIsom`,
  `stampTerrain`, `fillFlat`, `fillArea`, `replaceTerrain`, `tilesFromIsom`, `heal`) skip
  pinned tiles; `setTile`, `setTiles`, `placeBlend`, `paste` and the doodad calls write
  through.
- A plugin can hand the editor what it would otherwise learn or scan:
  `api.terrain.providePieces`, `provideTypes`, `provideBorrowTable`,
  `provideBorderTable` and `provideCopies`. A plugin's declaration stands ahead of a
  mod's `terrain.json`, and the file ahead of what was learned.
- `tx.triggers.fromText` throws on a mistake and changes nothing, and
  `api.triggers.text.parse` adds no strings when it throws. On success it answers real
  string indices as before.
- `@scm-js/core` reads mods through `@scm-js/mod` and exports the sheet, pieces and
  terrain file functions. Every export of 0.13.3 is kept, the old hash helpers as
  deprecated wrappers; `probeNames` alone changes shape.
- The bundled plugins move to Aftermath 0.26.0, scm-sim 0.21.0 and TrigScript 4.5.0.
  scm-sim's view says whether the game's own screen is offered and on (`ownScreen`,
  `ownScreenOn`), `options({ ownScreen })` chooses it, and `onLooks` hears every change.
- The API version stays 1. In an older editor, call an addition as `tx.heal?.(…)`.
